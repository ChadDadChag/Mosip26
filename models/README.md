## Why FaceMesh V2 and not the legacy 468-point mesh

The legacy `face_landmark.tflite` (468 points, 192×192) was used in Phase 1. Its eyelid contour
never closes, so blinks could not be detected. The webcam A/B below ran both meshes on the same
frames and the same ROI. It reports, per eye, the minimum or the hold median divided by the
open-eye median, using the contract's standard EAR points.

| Mesh | Open-eye EAR | Blink minimum / open | 2-s closed hold / open | ORT time per run |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| legacy 468 | 0.23–0.24 | 0.62–0.63 | 0.69 | 3.2 ms |
| **FaceMesh V2** | 0.29 | **0.04** | **0.16–0.22** | 9.4 ms |

Person 3's BlinkDetector needs closed < 0.6 × baseline and reopened > 0.85 × baseline. Only V2
meets that, with margin.

V2 contains only standard TFLite ops: CONV_2D, DEPTHWISE_CONV_2D, PRELU, ADD, MAX_POOL_2D, PAD,
LOGISTIC, RESHAPE, and DEQUANTIZE for the fp16 weights.

## ROI convention for the mesh

We checked MediaPipe's own source at commit `e9794ef55e2509c09aa8b6ba1764b797db8eb200`:
- **First frame** (`mediapipe/tasks/cc/vision/face_detector/face_detector_graph.cc`, lines
  240–253): rotation from detector keypoint 0 to 1 (the subject's right eye to their left eye),
  target 0°, rect scaled 1.5 × 1.5.
- **Tracking** (`mediapipe/tasks/cc/vision/face_landmarker/face_landmarks_detector_graph.cc`,
  lines 138–154): rotation from mesh point 33 to 263, scale 1.5, `square_long`.

We use the tracking form on the YuNet detection:
- a square of side 1.5 × max(w, h) around the box centre
- rotated so the YuNet eye keypoints are level
- bilinear sampling with replicated borders, to 256 × 256
- landmarks projected back through the same affine

## Head-pose pitch bias

The pose formula is unbiased: it gives −0.7° on MediaPipe's canonical face model. The mesh,
however, reports neutral, camera-facing heads about 9–10° "down".

`tools/calibrate_pitch.py` measures this over the 49 frontal official NASA portraits listed in
`tools/pitch_calibration_urls.txt`; the images themselves are not committed. The raw pitch
median is −9.45° and the IQR 6.26° (Q1 −12.36°, Q3 −6.10°).

The analyzer therefore reports `pitch = raw − PITCH_BIAS_DEG` with `PITCH_BIAS_DEG = −9.5`.
The golden JSON keeps the raw value as `pitchRaw`.

## Quality thresholds

`reference_pipeline.quality()` measures the primary face on a 112 × 112 axis-aligned crop of its
box, converted to BT.601 luma after bilinear sampling. These are initial values; Phase 5
calibrates them live.

| Issue | Rule |
|---|---|
| TOO_DARK / TOO_BRIGHT | mean luma < 40 / > 220; also TOO_BRIGHT when more than 30% of the face crop is clipped (R, G and B all ≥ 250). Webcam auto-exposure keeps the mean near 110 even when part of the face is blown out. |
| BLURRY | variance of the 4-neighbour Laplacian over the 110 × 110 interior (`lapVar`) < **15** |
| FACE_TOO_SMALL | face area < 4 % of the frame, or face width < 80 px |
| FACE_TOO_LARGE | face area > 60 % of the frame |
| FACE_OFF_CENTER | face-centre offset > 0.25 of the frame width or height |
| FACE_CUT_OFF | the box leaves the frame |

**Blur study (`lapVar`).** Each row was also measured after a Gaussian blur of the whole frame,
keeping the box from the sharp frame.

| Source | Face width | Sharp | σ = 1.5 | σ = 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| golden portrait / mirrored (554 × 640) | 164 / 156 px | 425.0 / 386.2 | 53.7 / 49.4 | 9.4 / 8.7 |
| golden crew primary / mirrored (640 × 512) | 51 / 52 px | 84.8 / 87.8 | 3.2 / 3.1 | 0.33 / 0.34 |
| webcam, 30 frames, dim room (luma 59–82) | 131–138 px | 41.0–61.2 (median 52.8) | not measured | not measured |
| webcam, 30 frames (luma 114–121) | 111–114 px | 34.4–65.3 (median 51.8) | 4.3–6.1 | 0.9–1.3 |

`T_blur` is the geometric mean of the lowest sharp value (34.4, webcam) and the highest
same-camera σ = 1.5 value (6.1): √(34.4 × 6.1) = 14.5, rounded to **15**. That leaves about
2.3× margin on each side.

`lapVar` is an absolute measure of detail at the 112-px scale. A σ = 1.5 blur of a high-resolution
face (the portrait, 53.7) therefore still reads sharper than a raw webcam frame. The threshold is
set for the capture domain that matters: live webcam frames.

`replay_cues()` computes two replay signals for every single-face frame:
- **moire:** log10(max / median) of the Hann-windowed 64 × 64 DFT magnitude over radii 8–31.
  The golden faces read 1.25–1.70; a live webcam reads 1.02–1.29.
  Since Phase 6b it feeds the **session moire cap** (`session_scores()`). When the rolling median
  of the last 10 single-face frames' moire is at least 1.40, over at least 3 values, the score is
  capped at 0.60. That sends a phone held close to the camera to a challenge; see
  `docs/part2-pad-architecture.md`.
- **glare:** the fraction of 112 × 112 crop pixels with R, G and B all ≥ 250. It is audit only. It
  is not in `padDetails`; tools read it through `OnnxFaceAnalyzer.lastReplayCues()`.

## Conversion checks

- **MiniFASNet.** The networks are built from the unmodified upstream `MiniFASNet.py` with
  `conv6_kernel=(5,5)`, `num_classes=3` and `embedding_size=128`. Weights are loaded with
  `strict=True` after stripping the `module.` prefix.
  - torch vs onnxruntime 1.31.0 on 20 random 0..255 inputs: max logit difference 2.6e-6 (V2) and
    2.9e-6 (V1SE). The pass limit is 1e-3.
  - Cross-check against yakhyo/face-anti-spoofing's release ONNX exports: max softmax[1] difference
    1.2e-7.
  - The export is byte-reproducible with torch 2.14.1.
- **FaceMesh V2.** TFLite interpreter (TF 2.15.1) vs onnxruntime 1.31.0 on 3 random inputs and on
  2 face crops built by the reference pipeline, comparing max differences per output:
  - `Identity` (landmarks): 3.4e-4 (limit 0.01)
  - `Identity_1` (presence logit): 7.6e-5 (limit 1e-3)
  - `Identity_2`: 2.2e-5
  - tf2onnx is **not** byte-reproducible, because re-running it renames the folded constants.
    The committed file is the canonical one. Its SHA-256 is recorded in the golden fixtures and,
    from Phase 4, in the signed manifest.
- **YuNet** is used exactly as published.

## Reproduce

Run from the repo root, using the two Python 3.11 venvs described in `tools/requirements*.txt`.

```
python -I models/tools/fetch_models.py --extras            # main env: download + verify sources, install YuNet
python -I models/tools/export_minifasnet.py --yakhyo       # main env: .pth -> ONNX + parity
python -I models/tools/make_golden.py --images-only        # main env: golden PNGs (if missing)
python -I models/tools/convert_facemesh.py inputs          # main env: mesh check inputs
python -I models/tools/convert_facemesh.py convert         # tf2onnx env: tflite -> ONNX + TFLite reference outputs
python -I models/tools/convert_facemesh.py verify          # main env: ONNX vs TFLite
python -I models/tools/make_golden.py                      # main env: golden JSON + assertions
python -I models/tools/calibrate_pitch.py                  # main env: re-derive PITCH_BIAS_DEG
python -I models/tools/inspect_models.py                   # main env: regenerate tools/model-io.md
python -I models/tools/sanity_webcam.py                    # main env, with a person at the camera
```

Downloads go to `models/tools/.cache/`, which is gitignored. A re-converted mesh will differ in
bytes from the committed one, so check it with `verify` and do not commit it unless the model
itself changed.

## The Python reference pipeline is the specification

`tools/reference_pipeline.py` defines every pre- and post-processing step that the Java analyzer
must reproduce: resampling, letterbox, YuNet decoding and NMS, the mesh ROI and back-projection,
head pose and pitch bias, the Silent-Face crop and the score fusion. Each formula is in the
docstring of its function.

`tools/make_golden.py` runs it on two public-domain PNG images and writes the expected values to
`liveness-analyzer/src/test/resources/golden/`. Those values include per-stage tensor statistics,
so a parity failure in Java can be traced to one stage.

## Integrity (signed bundle `pad-2026.10.1`)

`manifest.json` lists the four files with their sizes and SHA-256 hashes, and carries the mesh's
`pitchBiasDeg` calibration as a signed parameter. `manifest.sig` holds
`<keyId>:<hex DER ECDSA P-256 signature>` over the exact bytes of `manifest.json`.
`.gitattributes` keeps both files byte-exact.

At start-up the analyzer (`VerifiedBundle`, called by `OnnxFaceAnalyzerFactory`) does the
following:
- verifies the signature against a public key compiled into `TrustedKeys`, BEFORE parsing;
- checks every size and hash;
- builds the ONNX sessions from the same verified bytes.

Any problem gives `MODEL_INTEGRITY_FAILED`, and there is no fallback.

**The key is a development key**, `mosip-liveness-dev-2026-10`. Its private half lives only in
`%USERPROFILE%\.mosip-liveness\model-signing-key.p8` on the developer's machine and is never
committed. **Replace it with the production signing key before release.**

To re-sign after changing a model, use `liveness-eval`'s `ModelBundleTool`:

```
java -cp <liveness-eval classpath> io.mosip.registration.liveness.eval.ModelBundleTool \
     manifest --dir models --version pad-YYYY.MM.N --key-id <id> --param pitchBiasDeg=-9.5
     sign     --dir models --key %USERPROFILE%\.mosip-liveness\model-signing-key.p8 --key-id <id>
     verify   --dir models
```

Offline updates go through `ModelStore`:
- **install:** verify, anti-rollback, atomic switch, keep the previous bundle;
- **rollback:** an explicit operator action.

These are also available as `ModelBundleTool install|rollback`.
